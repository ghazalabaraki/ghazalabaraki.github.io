Case 02 · Digital health · Canada

A clinic that ran on paper and WhatsApp

Metamed is a physician-led metabolic health practice. Patient records, appointments, body composition data and every conversation lived in different places. They didn't want another tool. They wanted one product they owned.

01

It started when

A clinician said, in effect: everything is on paper and WhatsApp, and I can't see how my patients are actually doing.

That single sentence contained the whole problem. New patients filled in six pages of paper history that somebody then re-keyed. Conversations happened over WhatsApp, which meant they were scattered across personal phones and impossible to review. Body composition scans lived in the machine that produced them. Appointments lived somewhere else again.

Nobody could see a patient's whole journey in one place, least of all the clinicians responsible for it. Every hour spent reconciling systems was an hour not spent on care, and the practice couldn't grow past the limits of manual work.

The brief was consolidation. Not a new point solution to add to the pile, but one platform the practice owned, with the tools they already trusted pulled inside it.

02

What I owned

I ran this end to end: discovery through release, with a cross functional team across engineering, design and technical leadership. Ceremonies, backlog, quality validation and client reporting were mine.

  • Turned an open ended frustration into a defined scope: 32 epics, sequenced into 3 milestones, each one solving a complete workflow rather than scattering half features across all of them
  • Integrated what the practice already depended on rather than replacing it, so nobody had to relearn their own clinic
  • Owned the signup to payment flow: plan selection, consent capture, and payment at onboarding, with plans defining duration, schedule and included appointments
  • Defined the clinician side around the real care team, which set the permissions model for who reads and writes what
  • Prioritised secure patient to care team messaging with attachments, unread counts and notification behaviour, the details that decide whether a clinic adopts a chat feature or ignores it
  • Owned app store release as a regulated health product: classification, content rating, and the data safety declaration across every field the product collects
  • Ran QA and release readiness gates, validated with external testers before client handoff

MILESTONE 1

Access and onboarding

Secure login, digital intake forms replacing paper, in app messaging, and a basic clinician dashboard.

MILESTONE 2

Progress and plans

Body composition data pulled in automatically, a progress dashboard, and care plans clinicians could assign from templates.

MILESTONE 3

Launch readiness

Appointment management, direct access to the care team, end to end QA, and deployment.

03

Decisions and trade-offs

Every one of these cost something. That's what makes them decisions rather than preferences.

Take payment through Stripe, not in app purchase

The callProcess plan payments through Stripe at signup, and document that classification for app review before submitting. WhyMetamed sells real world clinical care: physician supervision, scheduled appointments, a multi month programme. Platform guidelines place services delivered outside the app, including medical consultations, outside in app purchase. Getting the classification right wasn't a preference, it was the condition of being approved at all. What it tookMapping the payment model to the right policy category, preparing a reviewer facing explanation, and providing a review path that completed signup without a live payment. Traded awayThe convenience of the platform payment sheet, and ownership of subscription mechanics, refunds and payment method changes.

Let existing patients move across without paying twice

The callA full discount onboarding path for patients already paying the clinic through its previous platform. WhyThe whole thesis was consolidation. If migrating meant being charged a second time for care they had already bought, patients wouldn't move, and the platform would launch empty. Traded awayAn extra step in onboarding, and a discount path that has to be controlled carefully.

Ship access and onboarding before anything clinical

The callMilestone 1 carried login, registration, digital intake and a basic dashboard. Progress tracking and care plans waited. WhyPaper was the pain the clinic could feel every single day. Solving it first meant they experienced the product working before the harder clinical features arrived, which bought trust for the rest of the build. Traded awayReal time chat sat in the same milestone and the client wanted it, but at eight points it would have consumed the first sprint and it needed accounts to exist first. It shipped in the sprint after.

Integrate the practice's tools rather than rebuild them

The callWire in the body composition system, payments, messaging and verification services rather than building equivalents. WhyThe clinicians already trusted this data. Consolidation meant unifying what they used, not replacing it and asking them to relearn their practice. Traded awayDependency on systems outside the team's control, and an integration surface that grows with every tool added.

05

What I'd do differently

Close the scope at the start, and stop entertaining scope creep.

I let scope stay open. Every new idea got a hearing, and a hearing is already a cost: it moves sequencing, reopens settled decisions, and turns a defined milestone into a negotiation. Milestone one shipped, but slower than it needed to, because the boundary around it kept moving.

What I'd change

Next timeAgree the scope of each milestone in writing, then close it. New requests go to the next milestone by default, not into a discussion about whether this one can absorb them. Why it works"Not now" is a far easier answer than "no", and it only exists if there is a written next milestone to put things in. Without one, every request becomes a decision about the current sprint. The honest partSaying yes felt like service. It wasn't. A client whose scope keeps growing is a client whose date keeps moving, and they feel that far more than they feel any individual feature they asked for.
